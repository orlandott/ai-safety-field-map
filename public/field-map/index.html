The AI Safety field, by the numbers

Each branch of AI safety research as a line: years across the bottom, headcount up the side. Press play (or drag the slider) to watch every branch grow over time.

People (FTEs) by branch (2025). Full-time-equivalent researchers, 2017–2025. The 2025 technical figures are taken directly from McAleese's org-by-org dataset (629 FTEs across 69 orgs, aggregated by category: misc technical ~200, interpretability 122, evals 100 — AISI ~50, METR ~31, Apollo ~19 — LLM safety 87, …). Non-technical 2025 is ~500: governance ~250, non-technical research ~120, advocacy/field-building ~60, ethics/other ~50. 2025 technical points are anchored to that dataset; every earlier year is modeled — technical history is reconstructed from each org's founding year and normalised to McAleese's published technical totals (≈30 in 2017, ≈50 in 2020, ≈300 in 2022), and the non-technical splits are modeled from his category totals (we don't yet have the org-level non-technical sheet). Every point carries its own source (see the per-year sourcing table below): 'anchor' = straight from the dataset, 'modeled' = our reconstruction. Per-bucket splits are approximate, so totals don't sum exactly.

Technical alignment
Misc technical safety 200 FTEs Broad technical safety work not tied to one sub-area — labs, nonprofits and academic groups (DeepMind, Apart, MATS, FAR, …).
Mechanistic interpretability 122 FTEs Reverse-engineering model internals: circuits, features, sparse autoencoders (Anthropic, Goodfire, Transluce, …).
Evals & dangerous-capability testing 100 FTEs Capability and propensity evals — bio, cyber, autonomy, persuasion (AISI ~50, METR ~31, Apollo ~19).
LLM safety 87 FTEs Safety work focused specifically on large language models (OpenAI, MIT AAG, academic groups, …).
RL safety 29 FTEs Reward modeling, reward hacking, specification gaming, goal misgeneralization (CHAI, Krueger lab, …).
AI security 21 FTEs Model-weight security, data poisoning, jailbreak/misuse pathways (Palisade, Gray Swan, Lakera, …).
Agent foundations 18 FTEs Decision theory, embedded agency, corrigibility (MIRI-lineage theory; CORAL, Dovetail, Orthogonal).
AI control 11 FTEs Containing misaligned-but-capable models — distinct from aligning them (Redwood, Luthien).
Multi-agent alignment 11 FTEs Cooperation, conflict and alignment among multiple AI agents (Conitzer, Softmax).
Interdisciplinary 10 FTEs Cross-disciplinary foundations bridging AI safety with other fields (PIBBSS, ACS).
AI forecasting 5 FTEs Scaling laws, compute trends, takeoff and timeline forecasting (Epoch AI).
Theoretical safety 4 FTEs Formal guarantees and theoretical safety research (ARC theory).
Other emerging areas 11 FTEs Smaller / newer technical areas: CoT monitoring, value learning, provably-safe AI, lock-in risk, brain-like AGI safety.
Governance & policy
Compute governance 53 FTEs (est.) Tracking/controlling training compute, export controls, on-chip mechanisms.
Policy & standards 114 FTEs (est.) Frontier-model regulation, evals-based policy, standards, law (EU AI Act, RSPs).
International coordination 53 FTEs (est.) Treaties, arms-race dynamics, international institutions for AI.
Lab governance & RSPs 30 FTEs (est.) Responsible scaling policies, internal lab governance and commitments.
Cross-cutting
Non-technical research 120 FTEs (est.) Non-technical AI-safety research: strategy, economics, security studies, sociotechnical analysis.
Advocacy & field-building 60 FTEs (est.) Public engagement, awareness, talent pipelines and field-building.
Ethics & societal impact 50 FTEs (est.) Fairness, bias, model welfare, and broader social impact of advanced AI.
